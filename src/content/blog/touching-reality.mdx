I think one of the biggest problems in modern life is that most people experience reality indirectly.

They watch instead of participate.

At some point I realized something embarrassingly obvious. Playing a video game is infinitely more meaningful than watching someone else play it.

Actually building a company feels different from endlessly consuming startup content.

Actually taking photos feels different from watching photography reels for three hours.

Actually shipping products feels different from reading productivity threads all day.

This sounds obvious on the surface, but modern life quietly pushes people into becoming permanent spectators because spectatorship is comfortable.

![Screenshot 2026-05-19](https://cdn.egeuysal.com/content/2026/05/19/asset-01.png)

## The Spectator Economy

The internet created the largest spectator economy in human history.

People spend thousands of hours watching:

- creators
- founders
- athletes
- gamers
- influencers
- streamers
- productivity creators
- self improvement content
- lifestyle content
- startup podcasts

A lot of people now experience ambition secondhand.

They consume the feeling of progress instead of making progress themselves.

That distinction matters more than most people realize because humans are not fulfilled by observation alone.

We want contact with reality. We want consequence, resistance, feedback, uncertainty, tension, and impact.

That is what touching reality means to me.

Not endlessly watching, preparing, optimizing, or simulating progress, but actually changing something outside yourself in a way that creates real consequence and feedback.

> A person touching reality leaves fingerprints somewhere.

A spectator consumes outcomes. An actor accumulates consequences.

## Self Improvement Became Another Form of Spectatorship

One of the strangest things about modern self improvement culture is how passive it often is.

People spend years consuming:

- dopamine detox videos
- discipline content
- productivity systems
- motivational clips
- optimization advice
- routines
- podcasts
- self improvement threads

Without ever entering reality themselves.

At some point self improvement stopped being improvement and became entertainment disguised as ambition.

I noticed this in myself too, especially during transition phases. When school ends, when routines collapse or when structure disappears.

You suddenly have infinite freedom and instead of acting, you drift into consumption because your brain mistakes stimulation for participation.

![Screenshot 2026-05-19](https://cdn.egeuysal.com/content/2026/05/19/asset-02.png)

The uncomfortable truth is that a flawed person touching reality is often more effective than an optimized person endlessly preparing.

Years ago people online treated quitting porn like it was the defining variable behind success.

Reality is more complicated than internet narratives. There are people with imperfect habits building incredible things every single day because they actually act.

Meanwhile other people build perfectly optimized systems around avoiding risk entirely.

Reality rewards contact. Not self perception.

## Why People Stay Spectators

Because touching reality is emotionally dangerous.

Reality can reject you. Your ideas can fail. People can laugh at your work. Your product can flop. Your writing can feel cringe.

Observation is psychologically safe while action exposes you to consequence.

I think this is why so many people stay spectators forever.

Not because they are incapable, but because spectatorship protects the ego from consequence, rejection, embarrassment, and failure.

If you only watch other people build things, your identity never gets tested against reality.

But the second you create something real, reality responds back. That changes you.

> Action forces negotiation with reality.

The internet normalized detached commentary.

Very few people intervene. Very few people actually touch reality directly.

## AI Will Amplify This Difference

Most people think AI will reduce the importance of humans.

I increasingly think the opposite is happening.

AI is reducing the cost of execution which means the real differentiators become:

- taste
- judgment
- agency
- emotional intuition
- conviction
- direction

Generation is becoming infinite. Selection is becoming valuable.

Anyone can generate:

- code
- UI
- writing
- logos
- videos
- startup ideas
- products

But very few people can recognize:

- what feels alive
- what resonates emotionally
- what should exist
- what actually matters
- what feels coherent

That is taste.

Taste is becoming one of the most valuable traits in the modern world because AI is still surprisingly weak at emotional coherence.

It can imitate style, execute patterns, and generate endless variations, but it still struggles with emotional intuition and the deeper human layer behind meaningful product decisions.

It does not feel obsession. It does not carry lived experience. It does not understand tension.

It does not know why certain products, ideas, or art pieces feel meaningful. That layer is still deeply human.

![Screenshot 2026-05-19](https://cdn.egeuysal.com/content/2026/05/19/asset-03.png)

The builders with strong taste will move faster than ever because AI amplifies them.

The spectators will fall even further behind because consumption becomes easier every year.

That is the real divide. Not humans versus AI. Actors versus spectators.

## Touching Reality

I think meaningful lives require contact with reality.

Not simulated achievement, endless preparation, or passive admiration, but real contact with reality through action, consequence, and participation.

Building something, changing something, or creating movement somewhere outside yourself through a product, a company, a photograph, a conversation, a piece of writing, an idea, or anything else that leaves evidence you existed beyond passive observation.

Anything that leaves evidence you were here. Because deep down, I do not think humans actually want infinite comfort.

I think humans want participation.

The strange thing is that reality almost immediately feels better than spectatorship.

Playing the game feels better than watching the stream, building feels better than endlessly consuming advice about building, and writing feels better than fantasizing about becoming a writer because participation fundamentally satisfies something observation never can.

Reality has texture in a way spectatorship never will because participation creates consequence, uncertainty, resistance, emotional attachment, and feedback loops that passive observation simply cannot replicate.

> The people who thrive are usually the people willing to touch reality directly even when it is uncomfortable, emotionally exposing, uncertain, and capable of rejecting them entirely.

Maybe that is what makes life meaningful in the first place.

Not watching triumph. Participating in it.
